How each chapter is built

Every chapter starts from a concrete problem, introduces each new term where it is first needed, works through examples, then steps back to the trade-offs. The boxed text:

A precise definition of a term the first time it is needed.

The idea from a section you should be able to state from memory.

A problem solved step by step.

A common mistake or confusing pair of terms.

Background or history that makes the main idea easier to understand. Lower exam priority.

How the topic is likely to be examined, and what a good answer contains.

Where to read more: chapter and section pointers into the course textbooks.

Each chapter ends with self-check questions, a slide-by-slide recap, and a table of key terms, all combined in the searchable Glossary.

A suggested way to revise

  1. Read a chapter end to end, then attempt its self-checks before opening the answers. Mark it revised at the bottom.
  2. Do the calculations by hand; use the interactive models to check, not to replace, your working.
  3. Build your cheat sheet as you go, starting from the formula sheet.
